Why would a marketing student write about supercomputers?

Why I made this

This past summer at WuXi AppTec, I helped launch Voyager, a customer intelligence platform. It turned one or two days of account research into under a minute. Then came the harder part. Sales reps wanted to get in and out fast. Marketing wanted every contact and project on record. We met in the middle: an account search that's easy to use, quick tags rather than extra fields, and a points game to get the records filled in.

What stayed with me is that people don't adopt a product because it's good. They adopt it when someone explains it in their terms. DGX is the same puzzle on a much bigger stage, because the person who signs off is rarely the person who runs it. So I tried writing for both.

Who signs off on a DGX purchase?

Three people, three different questions

An infrastructure leader asks whether the team can run it. A head of AI asks whether work built at the desk can move to the data center. A CFO asks what it takes to power it, and that question is getting louder: NVIDIA now calls energy "the biggest bottleneck for AI infrastructure buildouts."11

Buyers can also reach NVIDIA platforms through DGX, partner servers built on HGX or MGX, or the cloud,202122 so DGX messaging has to explain fit, not just speed.

Why Vera Rubin, storage and networking matter to buyers now

  1. Power. "Energy is now the biggest bottleneck for AI infrastructure buildouts."11 NVIDIA says DSX Max-Q enables "30% more AI infrastructure within a fixed-power data center" (no baseline stated by NVIDIA).19

    So what: buyers will compare tokens per watt, not just GPU counts.

  2. Storage. BlueField-4 STX is "AI-native storage infrastructure that extends GPU memory seamlessly across the POD", and NVIDIA DOCA Memos, which "enables dedicated KV cache storage processing", can "boost inference throughput by up to 5x" (versus general-purpose storage architectures).17

    So what: agents carry long context as KV cache. When STX holds that cache in a shared layer across the POD, storage becomes an inference-throughput decision, not a capacity line item.

  3. Networking. Spectrum-6 SPX "is engineered to accelerate east-west traffic across AI factories";18 the Vera Rubin DSX reference design spans "compute, NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet networking and storage".12

    So what: the network is now sold as part of the factory, not an add-on.

  4. Modular builds. Infrastructure is moving "from discrete chips and standalone servers to fully integrated rack-scale systems, POD-scale deployments".15 Partners such as Vertiv are building "a prefabricated, converged data center infrastructure solution" on DSX.14

    So what: the buying unit is the rack and the POD, so messaging should be too.

  • "BMW reports data scientist productivity up eight-fold." → "NVIDIA reports DGX boosted data scientists' productivity eight-fold at BMW." Wrong speaker: the figure is published by NVIDIA, not BMW.
  • "Hear how leaders such as Lockheed Martin and BMW are putting AI factories to work." → Cut. The webinar now promises a planning checklist instead. Unsupported: no source says these customers present.

The 8-rule checklist it produced

  1. Full name with "NVIDIA" at first mention
  2. "DGX SuperPOD", never "SuperPOD" alone
  3. Model names exactly as NVIDIA writes them
  4. "AI factory" is a concept, not a product
  5. Numbers verbatim, with their conditions
  6. Results credited to whoever published them
  7. Every number carries a source
  8. No invented offers, prices, specs or speakers

Questions I'd bring to PM and Engineering

  1. Under what workloads and cluster sizes was Mission Control's "85% power with 93% performance throughput" measured, and can sellers name them?10
  2. "Recover from problems 10x faster": faster than what baseline?9
  3. Which BlueField-4 STX and DOCA Memos KV-cache figures, such as the "up to 5x" inference throughput, can go into customer-facing material, and with which conditions?17
  4. How should DGX systems be positioned next to partner systems built on HGX Rubin NVL8, so partners read them as complementary?21
  5. The "up to 10x higher inference throughput per watt" figure for Vera Rubin NVL72: what is the comparison point we're allowed to state?16

Feedback I'd have to reconcile

Sales wants "up to Nx faster" in the headline

Rule 5 on this page says numbers stay verbatim, with their conditions. I'd headline the outcome and keep the number and its conditions in the proof line, the way the CFO one-pager leads with "Make every watt earn" and puts 85% / 93% in the body.

Partners want DGX and HGX framed as complementary; DGX marketing wants a reason to choose DGX

I'd lead with the line already in the guide, "Here NVIDIA tests the full stack together and stands behind it with its own AI experts and enterprise support", and never say "only". It gives DGX a reason without making partner systems the wrong choice.

Accurate isn't the same as persuasive

The LLM draft sounded like marketing and failed 8 of 35 claims. My first fix quoted NVIDIA so closely it read like a compliance review. The final version writes original, benefit-led copy and cites only the proof.

Check framing, not just numbers

The model never got a number wrong. It added claims, raised the volume, moved the speaker and shortened a name, so the checklist targets exactly those.
